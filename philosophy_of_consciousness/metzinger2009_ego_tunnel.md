## Summary

Metzinger argues that what we experience as a self is a transparent phenomenal self-model generated by the brain. We do not encounter this as a model, but as immediate reality, creating the impression of a substantial "I." The book combines philosophy of mind and cognitive science to argue for the "no-self" thesis at the level of phenomenal ontology while preserving rich functional accounts of self-related cognition.

## Authors

Thomas Metzinger is a philosopher of mind known for the self-model theory of subjectivity and influential work at the interface of phenomenology, cognitive science, and consciousness studies.


## Key concepts

- **Phenomenal self-model (PSM)** - representational model experienced as "me."
- **Transparency** - model structure is hidden from introspection and appears as reality.
- **No-self thesis** - no indivisible inner entity is required for self-experience.
- **Self-model dynamics** - identity is process-like, generated and updated over time.
