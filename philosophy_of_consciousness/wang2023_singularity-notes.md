# Self-awareness, a singularity of AI

**Authors:** Jinchang Wang  
**Year:** 2023  
**Citation key:** `wang2023_singularity`  
**PDF:** [DOI / publisher link](https://doi.org/10.17265/2159-5313/2023.02.003)

## Summary

Wang treats **self-consciousness** as a discontinuity in what software and robots can achieve. He frames the discontinuity as a mathematical singularity and argues that ordinary scaling ("more compute, more data") does not cross it. The paper surveys optimism about strong AI and Kurzweil's technological singularity before claiming that systems without human-like subjective self and self-conscious emotions remain **tools**. A late section argues that Kurzweil's singularity presupposes self-directed open-ended improvement, which the author denies absent machine self-awareness.

## Authors

Jinchang Wang is affiliated with Stockton University (School of Business). The article builds on his earlier pieces on **subjective self-identity**, **SCEs on robots**, and **constituents of subjective-self** (cited in the paper’s reference list).


## Key concepts

- **Subjective self** — first-person locus from which one “observes” the world; contrasted with third-person descriptions of cognition.
- **Singularity (two senses)** — mathematical odd point vs Wang’s claim of an **unachievable** boundary for AI; contrasted with Kurzweil’s **technological singularity**.
- **Self-conscious emotions (SCEs)** — emotions that presuppose self-representation (e.g. pride, shame); argued absent in robots in the author’s framework.
- **Tool vs species** — normative conclusion: advanced AI stays a **tool**; catastrophic risk framed through **misuse** and **designers**, not machine **dominance**.

## Notes

- The argument mixes **philosophy of mind**, **popular AI risk discourse**, and **theology-adjacent** language (“spirit and soul”); disentangle which claims are **empirical**, **definitional**, or **metaphysical** when citing.
- Useful counterpoints in the same reference block: **Searle**, **Penrose**, **Minsky**, **Kurzweil** — Wang positions against unbounded machine **personhood** and self-improving **autonomy**.
