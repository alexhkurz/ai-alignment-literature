## Summary

Decades of debate over **readiness-potential** experiments and their descendants anchor the piece: they appear to show brain activity preceding reported conscious urge or intention in simple motor tasks. The feature profiles Chapman neuroscientist Uri Maoz as pushing beyond highly artificial “raise your hand whenever” paradigms toward richer notions of **intention** and as collaborating with philosophers to keep terms like desire, urge, and commitment distinct. The reporting connects that narrative to existential unease about whether people are “passengers” in their own choices. It closes by noting Maoz’s interest in how similar questions might extend to **artificial agents** as autonomy increases.
