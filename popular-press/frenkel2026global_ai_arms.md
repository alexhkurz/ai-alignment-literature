## Summary

The piece describes an **intensifying military competition** among the United States, China, Russia, and others over autonomous drones, fighter coordination, and intelligence systems that lean on machine learning to act with less human oversight. Officials and industry voices compare the dynamic to the **early nuclear era**, but stress that AI-enabled weapons may be harder to bound, attribute, or govern than nuclear stockpiles were. The reporting highlights **speed and opacity**: classified budgets, rapid factory ramp-ups, and experiments—attributed to multiple governments—with delegating battlefield decisions further toward automation. Ethical debate about **meaningful human control** is portrayed as struggling to keep pace with procurement and deployment pressure.

## Authors

Sheera Frenkel (San Francisco), Paul Mozur (Taipei), and Adam Satariano (London) are *New York Times* correspondents covering technology, security, and international business.


## Key concepts

- **Autonomous weapons** and reduced human deliberation time in targeting chains.  
- **Mutually reinforcing rivalry** between major powers and spillover to smaller states’ programs.  
- **Governance gap** between nonbinding pledges and fielded capabilities.  
- **Industry role** (defence startups, dual-use supply chains) alongside traditional state labs.
