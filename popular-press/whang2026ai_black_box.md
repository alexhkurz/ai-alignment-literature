## Summary

The article contrasts “white box” systems such as Deep Blue, whose behaviour could in principle be read off explicit search and evaluation code, with modern deep networks whose competence emerges from **large-scale optimisation** over enormous parameter spaces. It uses AlexNet and later **language models** as milestones where useful performance outran engineers’ ability to give a faithful, fine-grained mechanistic story of what internal representations are doing. The reporting frames **interpretability** as a growing scientific agenda—sometimes likened to studying an unfamiliar natural phenomenon—motivated by the widening gap between societal reliance on models and independent scrutiny of their behaviour in high-stakes settings. Company and academic voices emphasise that **audit pressure** is not merely an academic puzzle when models touch medicine, justice, or defence.

## Key concepts

- **Black box** models vs hand-designed “transparent” pipelines.  
- **Interpretability** as an empirical field (circuits-style work, probing, sparse features) rather than a single fix.  
- **Scale** and emergent structure as obstacles to naïve introspection.  
- **Institutional risk**: high-stakes deployment without explanations stakeholders can independently check.
