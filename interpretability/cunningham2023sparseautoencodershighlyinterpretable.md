## Summary

Applies sparse autoencoders (SAEs) to language-model activations to recover interpretable, monosemantic directions. The learned sparse features are more interpretable than alternative directions by automated interpretability metrics, and can be used to identify the specific features causally responsible for behavior on the indirect-object identification task. The work frames SAEs as a scalable, unsupervised way to resolve superposition and a foundation for future mechanistic interpretability.

## Key concepts

- **Sparse autoencoders (SAEs)** — unsupervised decomposition of activations into sparse, interpretable features.  
- **Polysemanticity / superposition** — neurons represent multiple unrelated concepts; SAEs recover the underlying directions.  
- **Monosemantic features** — each learned direction corresponds to a coherent human-understandable concept.  
- **Causal attribution** — sparse features localize causes of specific model behavior.
