## Summary

Surveys sparse autoencoders (SAEs) as a tool for interpreting and understanding the internal mechanisms of large language models. The authors cover how SAEs address polysemanticity by decomposing superimposed features into interpretable components, and they discuss training methods, evaluation protocols, and applications such as model editing, control, and safety analysis. The paper provides an overview of the current landscape and identifies open challenges in scaling SAEs to larger models and more complex behaviors.

## Key concepts

- **Sparse autoencoders for LLM interpretability** — disentangle superimposed features.  
- **Polysemanticity and superposition** — why neurons activate for many unrelated concepts.  
- **SAE training and evaluation** — sparsity, reconstruction, and feature interpretability.  
- **Applications** — model editing, control, and mechanistic analysis.
