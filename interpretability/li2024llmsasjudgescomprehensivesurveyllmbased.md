## Summary

Surveys the use of large language models as judges or evaluators of other systems' natural-language outputs. It organizes the literature around functionality (why use LLM judges), methodology (how to construct evaluation systems), applications, meta-evaluation, and limitations. The paper provides a systematic definition of the LLM-as-judge paradigm, compares common evaluation protocols, and discusses where LLM judges are reliable or brittle.

## Key concepts

- **LLM-as-judge** — using an LLM to score, rank, or critique natural-language outputs.  
- **Meta-evaluation** — evaluating the judge itself against human judgments or gold labels.  
- **Methodology** — prompting strategies, reference-based vs. reference-free, pointwise and pairwise evaluation.  
- **Limitations** — position bias, verbosity bias, self-enhancement, domain dependence.
