## Summary

Volume I of Hörmander's treatise on distribution theory and Fourier analysis. The opening theorem (often called the mean-value inequality) states that for a Banach-space-valued map \(f\) on an open interval, \(\|f(y)-f(x)\|\) is bounded by \(\|y-x\|\) times the supremum of \(\|f'(t)\|\) on the segment—without requiring \(C^1\) regularity beyond differentiability a.e. HeRA cites this result when extending the mean-value inequality to multivariate maps on rectangles.

## Key concepts

- **Mean-value inequality** — Lipschitz control from derivative supremum.  
- **Banach-space-valued functions** — proof by continuity method on \([0,1]\).  
- **Distribution theory / Fourier analysis** — broader context of the series.
